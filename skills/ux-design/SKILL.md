# UX design

Use when a page feels confusing, a flow has drop-offs, or a new user journey is needed. Read [strategy](../../knowledge/strategy.md) and [UX and accessibility](../../knowledge/ux-accessibility.md).

1. Define a specific user, entry point, goal and constraint. Walk the flow from arrival to result; note where intent, next action or feedback is unclear.
2. Prioritize content that helps decisions; write specific labels and honest expectations. Group navigation by visitor goals and make routes/anchors predictable; consult the navigation-architecture skill for menu states. Remove unnecessary steps and request data only when needed.
3. Design loading, empty, success and recoverable error paths. Preserve input and explain what to fix; make costs and consequences explicit.
4. Test the task with realistic content and representative devices, observe hesitation and failures, and distinguish observed evidence from conjecture.

Example: In a booking flow, show availability and total cost before asking for account creation; after a failed payment, retain booking details and offer a retry path. Deliver a journey map, copy/interaction changes, edge-state inventory and measurable task outcome. Do not optimize clicks at the expense of informed choice.
