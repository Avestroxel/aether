# Category-aware direction prompts

These are **decision lenses, not templates**. Identify the visitor's task and the brand's evidence first; then compare at least two possible structures. Adapt to existing products and content. Do not force a hero, card grid or prescribed color on any category.

| Category | Primary design tension | Direction to consider | Failure to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Landing page | Fast comprehension vs evidence | Lead with the user's outcome, then proof and one clear action | Abstract claim + decorative hero + repeated CTAs |
| SaaS marketing | Explain capability vs reduce evaluation risk | Demonstrate a specific workflow and limits; reveal pricing honestly | Identical feature-card sections with no product evidence |
| SaaS dashboard | Density vs scanning | Prioritize decisions, meaningful defaults and exception states | Marketing-site composition in a data tool |
| E-commerce | Desire vs purchase confidence | Product photography, specs, delivery/returns and predictable checkout | Motion obscuring stock, price or add-to-cart |
| Portfolio | Personal voice vs project proof | Outcome-led case studies with role and constraints | Image gallery with no process or contribution |
| Creative agency | Character vs credibility | Typography/image direction built from actual work | Experimental navigation hiding contact/work |
| Corporate | Trust vs complexity | Clear IA, governance, accessible documents | Arbitrary visual novelty obscuring essential information |
| Local business | Warmth vs findability | Hours, location, offerings and real imagery early | Generic testimonial claims without verifiable context |
| Web app | Flexibility vs learnability | Clear states, navigation and progressive disclosure | Decorative onboarding delaying the task |
| Admin panel | Efficiency vs error prevention | Dense but readable tables, safe destructive actions | Hero-like empty space in operational flows |
| Product launch | Excitement vs explanation | Show product mechanics and a concrete use case | Cinematic animation with no actionable information |
| Startup | Ambition vs proof | Audience-specific value and credible early evidence | Invented metrics or fake logos |
| Editorial / magazine | Expressive voice vs reading | Strong type, article rhythm and clear source hierarchy | Oversized display type in long-form body text |
| Education | Motivation vs clarity | Curriculum paths, progress and accessible practice | Gamification masking learning outcomes |
| Entertainment | Immersion vs orientation | Genre-appropriate atmosphere with obvious content discovery | Autoplay sound/motion without control |
| Interactive experience | Exploration vs completion | Give a clear invitation, status and escape route | Essential content trapped behind gesture-only interactions |
| 3D product experience | Spatial understanding vs load cost | Scene only where shape or interaction teaches something | Canvas-only navigation or missing static fallback |
| Experimental site | Surprise vs usability | Put experimentation in presentation, retain semantic controls | Accessibility sacrificed for novelty |

When a category crosses another (e.g. editorial commerce), combine the *tasks*, not the visuals. Define what must be apparent in the first viewport, which content provides proof, what can be deferred, and where interaction helps. Test the result with the brand substitution check in [distinctiveness](distinctiveness.md) and the QA matrix in [resources and QA](resources-qa.md).
