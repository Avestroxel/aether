# Visual direction

## Establish an unmistakable visual thesis

Every memorable website is governed by a coherent **art direction thesis**—three concrete attributes that dictate typography, surface materiality, spatial tension, and visual evidence:
- *Luxury Architecture Studio:* **Monolithic** (dramatic serif scale, asymmetric structural columns), **Tactile** (warm travertine and raw linen surfaces, exposed `1px` graphite hairlines), **Indexical** (tabular project numbers `01 — 06`, coordinates, material specs, elevation drawings).
- *Technical AI / Developer Platform:* **Precision-Engineered** (crisp grotesk + tabular mono, exposed grid hairlines), **Atmospheric** (multi-tiered obsidian surfaces with subtle specular top-borders), **Demonstrative** (live interactive workflow or inspectable code/telemetry artifact in the hero).
- *Editorial / Cultural Publication:* **Literary** (high-contrast display serif with drop caps and kickers), **Print-Structured** (column rules, marginalia, pull quotes), **Curated** (art-directed crop ratios and detailed captions).

## True minimalism vs. sterile emptiness

A common failure in AI-generated websites is confusing *minimalism* with *emptiness*:
- **Sterile emptiness (FORBIDDEN):** Unstyled white background, small system-font text, sparse one-line bullet points, missing imagery/diagrams, and large expanses of accidental blank space with no structural tension.
- **Architectural minimalism (REQUIRED when restraint is called for):**
  - **Sculptural proportion:** Extreme, deliberate contrast between monumental display typography (`clamp(2.75rem, 6vw, 5.5rem)`) and crisp, quiet metadata (`0.75rem` tracked uppercase or tabular mono).
  - **Tactile surface contrast:** Warm or cool tinted neutrals (`#F5F2EB` alabaster vs. `#EAE4D8` stone, or `#0B0C10` obsidian vs. `#13151C` graphite) separated by razor-sharp `1px` hairlines (`rgba` or `color-mix`).
  - **Intentional negative space:** Open canvas acts as an active structural frame that isolates and elevates a focal photograph, bespoke SVG architectural diagram, or typographic statement.

## Content and visual artifact authenticity

How you populate content and visuals depends on the project context:

1. **Existing product or repository with real data:**
   - Use the repository's verified product facts, actual terminology, real metrics, and existing assets.
   - Never fabricate false legal terms, fake security certifications (e.g., invented SOC2 badges), or fictional customer testimonials.
2. **New website or short prompt (greenfield creative build):**
   - Never punish a short prompt by outputting barebones wireframes, meta-notes (*"Insert real project photos here"*), or generic filler (*"Feature 1"*, *"Transform your workflow"*).
   - Author **domain-authentic, concrete, high-craft content**: realistic project names, geographic locations, material specifications, architectural dimensions, realistic workflow steps, or realistic domain datasets.
   - Pair copy with **purposeful visual artifacts**: bespoke, high-precision inline SVG diagrams (architectural floor-plan/elevation schematics, system topology graphs, typographic specimen plates, interactive data visualizations) or carefully art-directed, domain-matched imagery with intentional aspect ratios (`4:5` portrait, `16:10` architectural wide, `1:1` detail crop), subtle tonal grading, and descriptive `alt` text.

## Multi-scale visual audit

1. **Thumbnail scale (squint test):** Does the page have an unmistakable silhouette, dominant focal point, and varied section rhythm—or does it blur into a monotonous stack of centered boxes?
2. **Reading scale:** Do type hierarchy, contrast, and spacing guide the eye effortlessly from hook to evidence to action?
3. **Detail scale:** Are borders, alignment edges, icon strokes, focus rings, and interactive hover/active states crisp and cohesive?
